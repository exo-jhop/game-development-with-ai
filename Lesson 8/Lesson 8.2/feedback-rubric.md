# Lesson 8.2: Peer Feedback Guide

---

## Overview

This is a guide for giving constructive feedback during demo day. You are not grading anyone -- you are helping a fellow developer see their game through fresh eyes. Good feedback is specific, honest, and kind.

**Your job:** Watch the presentation, play the game if possible, and fill out this feedback sheet for each classmate whose game you review.

---

## How to Give Good Feedback

Before you start, keep these principles in mind:

1. **Be specific.** "The game was fun" is nice but not useful. "The dash mechanic felt responsive and I liked using it to dodge enemies" gives the developer something to build on.
2. **Lead with what works.** Always start with what you genuinely enjoyed. This is not flattery -- it tells the developer what to keep and expand.
3. **Frame suggestions as possibilities.** "What if the jump had a little screen shake?" is easier to hear than "The jump felt weak."
4. **Focus on the experience, not the code.** You are reviewing the game as a player, not auditing the source code.
5. **One suggestion per category is enough.** You do not need to find something wrong in every area. If the audio is great, say so and move on.

## Feedback Sheet

**Game Title:** ___________________________________

**Presenter:** ___________________________________

**Your Name:** ___________________________________

---

### 1. Game Feel

_How do the controls feel? Is the game responsive to input? Does movement feel satisfying?_

**What worked well:**

_[Write what felt good about playing the game. Was the character responsive? Did actions feel immediate? Was the difficulty fair?]_

**One suggestion for improvement:**

_[Is there a control that felt off? A delay that was noticeable? A mechanic that was confusing to use?]_

---

### 2. Visual Style

_Is there a consistent look? Can you tell what things are? Is the screen readable during gameplay?_

**What worked well:**

_[What caught your eye? Did the colors work together? Could you easily distinguish the player, enemies, collectibles, and background? Was there a particular visual moment that stood out?]_

**One suggestion for improvement:**

_[Was anything hard to see? Did any elements blend into the background? Were there visual elements that felt inconsistent with the rest of the game?]_

---

### 3. Audio

_Does the sound design add to the experience? Is there music? Sound effects?_

**What worked well:**

_[Did the music fit the mood? Were sound effects satisfying? Did audio cues help you understand what was happening in the game?]_

**One suggestion for improvement:**

_[Was anything too loud or too quiet? Were there actions that felt like they should have had a sound but did not? Did any sounds feel out of place?]_

---

### 4. Creativity

_Is the concept interesting? Does the game try something you have not seen before, or put a fresh spin on something familiar?_

**What worked well:**

_[What was the most original or interesting aspect of this game? Was there a mechanic, theme, or idea that surprised you?]_

**One suggestion for improvement:**

_[Was there an area where the game felt generic? Is there an opportunity to push the unique concept further?]_

---

### 5. Polish

_Does the game feel complete? Is there a beginning, middle, and end? Does it feel like a finished experience?_

**What worked well:**

_[Did the game have a clear start and goal? Were there menus, transitions, or UI elements that made it feel polished? Did the game loop feel complete?]_

**One suggestion for improvement:**

_[Was there something that felt missing -- a game-over screen, a restart option, instructions, a pause menu? Was there a rough edge that stood out?]_

---

### Overall Impressions

**What was the best part of this game?**

_[One thing that stood out as the highlight]_

**If this developer had one more week, what should they work on first?**

_[Your single highest-priority suggestion -- the one change that would make the biggest difference]_

**Anything else you want to tell them?**

_[Optional -- an encouragement, a question, an idea, whatever comes to mind]_

---

## Feedback Etiquette Reminders

- Everyone in the room built a game in the same timeframe. Respect the effort.
- Frame feedback as a fellow developer, not a critic. You are on the same team.
- If you cannot think of a suggestion in a category, write "Nothing -- this area was solid." That is valid feedback.
- Do not compare games to each other. Each project has different goals and scope.
- Handwriting is fine. Legibility is appreciated.
- Hand your completed feedback sheets to the presenter after demo day, or submit them digitally if the instructor provides a form.
