# Lesson 8.2: Game Demo Day -- Presentation Template

---

## Overview

This template helps you structure a 5-to-8-minute presentation for demo day. You will introduce your game, show it live, and share what you learned building it. Fill in each section below and use it as your speaking outline.

---

## Section 1: Opening (30 seconds)

**Game Title:**
_[Your game's name]_

**One-Line Pitch:**
_[Describe your game in a single sentence. What is it and why is it interesting?]_

Example: "Starfall is a top-down shooter where every enemy you defeat becomes your ally for the next wave."

**Your Name:**
_[Introduce yourself briefly]_

> Tip: Start with your pitch, not "Hi, my name is..." Lead with the game. The pitch is your hook -- make the audience want to see it.

---

## Section 2: The Concept (1 minute)

Answer these three questions:

**What kind of game is it?**
_[Genre, perspective, core mechanic. Example: "A 2D puzzle platformer with gravity-switching mechanics."]_

**Who is it for?**
_[Casual players? Fans of a specific genre? People who like short sessions? Challenge seekers?]_

**What inspired it?**
_[A game you love? A mechanic you wanted to explore? A theme that interested you? An idea that came up during brainstorming?]_

> Tip: Keep this brief. The audience wants to see the game, not hear a design document.

---

## Section 3: Live Demo (2-3 minutes)

This is the core of your presentation. Plan exactly what you will show.

**Planned Demo Path:**

1. _[Starting point -- where will the game be when you switch to it? Title screen? A specific level?]_
2. _[First thing to show -- the core mechanic in action]_
3. _[Second thing to show -- a challenge, a level, or a feature that adds depth]_
4. _[Third thing to show -- the most polished or impressive moment in your game]_
5. _[Ending -- how will you wrap up the demo? A death? A victory? Returning to the menu?]_

**Key Moments to Highlight:**

- _[Moment 1: What should the audience notice here?]_
- _[Moment 2: What makes this part interesting or fun?]_
- _[Moment 3: What are you most proud of in the game?]_

**What to Talk About While Playing:**

- Narrate what you are doing and why. "I'm going to grab this power-up, which switches gravity..."
- Point out things the audience might not notice. "See how the background parallax changes as I move?"
- If something goes wrong, keep talking. "That was not intentional, but let me show you..."

---

## Section 4: Development Story (1-2 minutes)

**Biggest Challenge:**
_[What was the hardest part of building this game? A mechanic that did not work? A bug that took hours? Scope that had to be cut?]_

**How AI Helped:**
_[Give one or two specific examples. "I used AI to help me write the enemy state machine." "AI helped me debug a physics issue that I had been stuck on for a day." "I generated placeholder art with AI to prototype faster."]_

**What You Learned:**
_[Pick your top insight. A technical skill, a design lesson, a process realization. Example: "I learned that playtesting early saves more time than polishing early."]_

**What You Would Do Differently:**
_[If you started over, what would change? "I would scope smaller." "I would build the UI first." "I would playtest with others sooner."]_

## Live Demo Safety Tips

Live demos break. It is not a question of if but when. Prepare for it.

### Before Demo Day

- [ ] Export a fresh build the night before. Do not demo from the development environment.
- [ ] Play through your entire demo path at least twice on the machine you will present from.
- [ ] Close all other applications. Free up RAM and CPU.
- [ ] Disable notifications on your computer (Do Not Disturb mode).
- [ ] Set your game to windowed mode at a resolution that works on the projector (1280x720 is safe).
- [ ] If you are demoing a web build, pre-load it in the browser. Do not rely on loading speed during the presentation.

### Have a Backup Plan

- **Record a backup video.** Use screen recording software (OBS, Windows Game Bar, or a screen capture tool) to record yourself playing through the demo path. If the live demo crashes, you can play the video instead.
- **Prepare screenshots.** If both the game and the video fail, you can still walk through screenshots and explain the game.
- **Know how to restart quickly.** If the game crashes, can you get back to a playable state in under 10 seconds? Practice this.

### During the Demo

- **Do not apologize for your game.** If something is rough, just show it. The audience knows this is a workshop project.
- **Narrate everything.** Silent gameplay is awkward for an audience. Talk about what you are doing.
- **Keep it short.** A focused 2-minute demo is better than a rambling 5-minute one.
- **If it breaks, move on.** Say "Let me skip to another part" or switch to your backup. Do not debug live.

|----------------------|-------------|

## Presentation Checklist

- [ ] One-line pitch is written and practiced
- [ ] Demo path is planned and tested
- [ ] Backup video is recorded
- [ ] Development story is prepared (challenge, AI usage, lessons learned)
- [ ] Q&A answers are drafted
- [ ] Build is exported and tested on the presentation machine
- [ ] Notifications are silenced on the presentation computer
- [ ] You have practiced the full presentation at least once out loud

---

## Reminder

Demo day is about sharing what you built, not proving it is perfect. Every game here was made in the same timeframe. Show your work, tell your story, and celebrate shipping something real.
